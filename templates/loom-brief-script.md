# Loom brief script

The words for the video a booked prospect gets before the meeting. Jonathan, 2026-10-05: "the loom scripts are going to be a script as well." Built from his two Momentum Looms (the review, 3:16, and the rebuilt page, 3:41) and his cut rules (`references/content-rules.md`). Fill the slots, record in one take, send the link on the confirmation's thread. The CRM ticks the Loom step when a sent email to them carries a loom.com link; the variation goes on the company's file (`projects/crm/looms.json`).

Rules, from his cuts:
- Never call them a prospect. Say "your page", "your lighting page".
- No guess about them stated as fact ("I'm assuming this is ChatGPT" went).
- Say only what is true of what is on screen. A render's form that sends nowhere is not "sent to my inbox".
- No underselling ("didn't take much time"), no "had a great time", no close that trails off.
- One point once. Reviews by the call to action is said one time.
- Under five minutes for the owner's version. The screen does the work; the form being filled stays whole.
- No price. The price is said on the call.

## The SOP (Jonathan, 2026-10-06: "What is a standard SOP for making loom videos")

Ten steps, about 40 minutes a video without a rebuilt page, about two hours with one.

1. **Read the company once.** `/research` site read and Google profile pull. Pick the one page the video is about and write down three changes. 15 minutes.
2. **Pick the variation.** A: the page reviewed. B: the page reviewed and rebuilt; the rebuilt render comes first (`/landing-page`, their brand, open in a second tab).
3. **Fill the script.** The slots below, printed or on a second screen. Five beats, not a read-aloud.
4. **Set up.** Loom desktop, screen plus camera bubble, 1080p, mic checked. Browser at 100 percent, one window, notifications off, their page loaded and scrolled to the top.
5. **Record in one take.** Open with their first name and what they will see. Talk to one person. Under five minutes. A flub: stop, two seconds of silence, say the sentence again; the silence is the cut point.
6. **Cut.** Trim the dead start and end in Loom, or hand it to `/social-content` for the owner's cut: ums, repeats, and any line that is not true of what is on screen come out, with a log beside the file.
7. **Title it plainly.** "{Company}: your {service} page, reviewed." Never "prospect", never "lead".
8. **Loom settings.** Anyone with the link can view. Notify on view (that is the "watched" signal). Comments on.
9. **Send it the same day it is booked**, on the confirmation's thread, at least a day before the meeting. The CRM ticks the Loom step when the sent email carries the loom.com link. Paste the link on the company's file with its variation; the length is read from Loom.
10. **Track it.** Mark watched from Loom's view email. Log show or no-show after the meeting. Reports reads show rate and close rate by variation; change one thing per variation, about 20 meetings each before reading a difference.

After the meeting: the owner's cut can become social content (the before-and-after short, the long form) once the company says yes to that on the call.

## Variation B: the page, reviewed and rebuilt (about 4 minutes)

### 1. Open (15 seconds)

> {First name}, this is your {service} page as it is today, and the same page rebuilt. Four minutes: what I changed and why. Then two things on the call.

### 2. The page as it is (60 to 90 seconds)

Say what is on screen, top to bottom. One line each.

> First screen. The headline says "{their headline}". That is {N} services on one page. One page, one service.
> The photo is {stock / dark / small}. {What a buyer sees first.}
> The button says "{their button}" and sits {where}. {What it leads to.}
> Below: {the body copy, how much of it}. The reviews sit {where}. {About / portfolio: there or not.}
> The last thing on the page is "{their footer ask}".

### 3. The rebuilt page (90 to 120 seconds)

Same order. For each change: what it is, then why, in one line.

> Headline: "{Service} installation in {area}". One service, the outcome, the place.
> Eyebrow above it: "{eyebrow}". It speaks to the person landing here.
> Photo: {the new picture}, high contrast, white text on dark.
> One button, centered, in your brand color: "Book a meeting".
> It opens a four-step form: first name, email, phone, message. {Fill it in on screen. Say nothing while the screen does the work.}
> Reviews moved up beside the button. Trust right before the action.
> Three cards: {control, automation, security}. One line each, an icon each.
> About: {the owner, the town}. A photo here does a lot of work.
> Second button at the bottom: "Book a meeting". Nobody scrolls back up.

### 4. What happens after the form (one line, only what is true)

> On the live page this form reaches a person who calls back {the same hour / the same day}.

Say the time only if it will be true for them. On a render, say "on the live page".

### 5. The close (15 seconds)

> That is the page. On the call: {two things, for example: the search terms buyers in {area} type, and what this page costs to run}. {Day} at {time}, {their zone}. Same link as the invite.

No sign-off line. Stop.

## Variation A: the full page review (about 11 minutes)

The first one made (Momentum, 2026-10-04, "Boosting Leads With High Converting Landing Pages"). Sections 1, 2, and 5 above, with section 2 run long: every block on the page, what it does, what would change. No rebuilt page. Use when there is no time to build one.

## Reading which variation works

Each company's file carries the variation it got (A or B) and whether they watched it. Reports, "Loom briefs" sets show rate and close rate by variation beside the meetings that got none. About 20 answered meetings a variation before a difference is read. Change one thing at a time.
