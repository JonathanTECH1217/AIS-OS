Timing

Each bar is split into sixteenths. Drag a syllable on the strip, or drag its note left or right on the staff, and it snaps to the ticks on the timing lines; the blue band shows where it will land. Notes never overlap: whatever a dropped note runs into is pushed just far enough, and dropping inside an earlier note lands right after it. Whatever stays empty becomes a rest.

Tools

M Move, E Edit, C Cut. Holding the right mouse button on a word drags it with Move whatever tool is active. Cut: click a word on the strip to split it where you clicked, or a note on the staff to split it in half. Right-click a word or note for a menu.

The note

Click a syllable or its notehead and the note turns blue; the status bar shows its pitch, degree, and length. Drag it up or down on the staff to set the pitch. In the Drums view the same drag moves it between the drum voices. 1 to 7 set the scale degree. In the Vocals view 1 is do and 7 is ti. In the other views 1 is the key's tonic, so in A minor 1 is A. Rests are the gaps between notes and never turn into notes. Clicking a rest picks the note before it, and / holds that note into the rest. Double-click an empty spot on a bar to add a note there. Ctrl+← Ctrl+→ move it a sixteenth in time, Ctrl+Shift an eighth. Ctrl+↑ Ctrl+↓ move it one staff step, Ctrl+Shift two steps. Shift+↑ Shift+↓ add a sharp or flat. Alt+↑ Alt+↓ shift the octave. Or drag the notehead up and down the staff.

Length

Drag the right edge of a note on the strip, or press + for longer and - for shorter, through sixteenth, eighth, quarter, half, whole. / adds a sixteenth, . takes one away; with Ctrl they add or take an eighth. ' dotted, 0 no note. A length with no single written value, like a quarter plus a sixteenth, draws as tied notes. A note can also be held past the bar line, up to four bars, and is written tied across it. Making a note longer pushes the notes after it later, never on top of them. Making it shorter leaves a gap, which is a rest.

Moving around

← → move to the word before or after, ↑ ↓ to the nearest word on the line above or below. Ctrl+← Ctrl+→ move the note in time by one Snap step, Ctrl+Shift two steps, Shift alone a sixteenth, Alt+[ and Alt+] an eighth. A moved note pushes only what it runs into. Alt+← Alt+→ move the whole rest of the line with it. Enter edits the text, Delete removes the word. Alt on its own opens and closes the tools drawer.

Shove

Alt+→ pushes the selected note and everything after it later by one Snap step. Alt+← pulls them earlier, stopping at the note before. Ctrl+Alt+← Ctrl+Alt+→ shove by a sixteenth, Ctrl+Alt+Shift by an eighth. Backspace removes up to a quarter of rest in front of the selected note; press it again for the next quarter.

Linked syllables

The syllables of one word stay linked: one box on the strip, and con-se-quence under the notes on the staff. Ctrl+Backspace on any note of the word, or Unlink in its right-click menu, takes the word apart into single notes. The Cut tool on a linked note frees just that one.

Solfege

D R I F S L T set do re mi fa sol la ti (I is mi, since M is the Move tool) and move on to the next syllable. The note spells itself from the key.

Drums

In the Drums view: K kick, N snare, A hat, O open hat, P pedal, H crash, I ride, T cycles the toms.

Getting the tempo right

A whole-number tempo is up to half a beat per minute off a real song, which is a full beat by the end of it, so the sheet keeps decimals. Tap gives a rough value; more taps, tighter number. Then Line up on the first word to place bar 1. Then, with the song playing, press B or Beat here on any beat well into the song: the tempo is set so the beat the band is nearest to lands exactly there, bar 1 unmoved. The further in, the more exact, as long as the band is still within half a beat of the song, so do it within the first minute and repeat later if you like. With Spotify the song's position is an estimate, so expect a tenth of a beat per minute of slack. With an attached MP3, Detect reads the tempo off the beats in the waveform. The clock button always shows the sheet's tempo; a small percent badge appears only when the band runs slower.

Lock. A straight tempo only fits a song that was recorded to a click. For one that breathes, press Lock after Line up: the app finds every beat in the song and the sheet follows them bar by bar, so the metronome stays on the drums all the way through. It reports how many beats sit on a hit and how far off they are, before and after. With an attached file this reads the waveform. With Spotify on the desktop copy it is automatic: the local server reads the computer's own sound output while the song plays through the app, and the beats lock in as it goes, settling within the first quarter minute and tightening to the end of the song. You still Line up once so it knows where bar 1 is. It needs two Python packages, installed once with pip install pyaudiowpatch numpy; without them, Listen asks to share the screen's audio instead. Either way only a loudness curve is kept, never the sound. Unlock, a new tempo, or Line up returns the sheet to a straight tempo.

Place the words

Place words, under Play, fills the sheet from the song in one press on the desktop copy. It looks up timed lyrics for the linked or attached song from LRCLIB, a free open lyrics database, and writes them onto the sheet as notes, replacing any words already there (a syllable whose text still matches keeps its notes). It reads the tempo and the time signature from the song's beats, puts bar 1 on a downbeat, locks to the beats, and then puts every syllable where the singer sings it, for as long as it is sung: the voice the app heard while the song played through (or the attached file's voice band) gives the onsets and the releases, and the gaps draw as rests. Before the song has been heard once the syllables are spread evenly across each line and the tempo stays as set. A song goes on a sheet once; after that the sheet is yours to edit (Ctrl+Z takes the whole fill back). Then Tap words fixes any syllable that landed wrong, and the pitches are yours.

Tap the words

Once the sheet is lined up (and locked, for a song that breathes), press Tap words under Play. The song plays and one word at a time is outlined in orange. Press a digit 1 to 7 the moment you hear it sung: the word lands on the nearest sixteenth of the beat with that scale degree, the word before it is stretched to last until it, and the outline moves on. Space places the word without changing its pitch. 0 ends the last word right there, so a breath becomes a rest. The first word of a line also sets where that line's bars begin, so a line that runs long or short in the song gets the right number of bars.

A digit means the nearer of the two notes with that name. After do on C4, pressing 5 gives the sol just above (G4). If the tune leaps down to the sol below, press − then 5; press + first for a leap up. Enter pauses the song; Enter again picks it up a bar before the outlined word, so a fast song can be taken in pieces. Backspace steps back one word to tap it again, Esc stops, and one Undo takes the whole pass back. Outside a pass, a digit on a selected note also moves the selection to the next note, so 1 3 5 5 fills four notes in a row.

Tuning and tones

The key button holds the tuning: A4 is 440 Hz unless you change it, and every played note keeps its equal-tempered ratio to that A, so a sheet at 432 or 442 plays true. Hear A sounds the reference. Under Play, the Tones panel has one key for each half step above do in the current key, with the scale degrees marked. Click one to hear it. With From do on, each tone is played after do, then both together, so you hear the interval. The octave picker moves the whole row.

Views

Pick the instrument in the top row, or press Tab to cycle through them (Shift+Tab backwards). Vocals shows solfege over the notes. Guitar shows the scale degree, 1 to 7, over each note and the note name in the key under the staff. Bass shows note names with tab. Piano shows both hands. Drums shows the drum staff with rhythm syllables over each hit: ta ka di mi for the four sixteenths of a beat, ta di for eighths.

Play

Space plays or pauses. The blue band follows the tempo and the page keeps it centered. Metronome clicks every beat when on.

Spotify

On the desktop copy: make a free app at developer.spotify.com with the redirect URI http://127.0.0.1:8765/callback, paste its Client ID under Play, press Connect, log in, then type the song's name in the search box and press Find. Pick it from the list (or paste a Spotify link instead) and it becomes the sheet's song with Audio switched on, so Play drives your Spotify at the right spot while the band follows it. Premium is required, and Spotify always plays at full speed. Once a song is linked a strip appears under the tabs: a click on it puts the song at that spot, the red line is where the song is and can be dragged, and the blue flag is where bar 1 of the sheet starts. On the sheet a red line shows where the song or band is and follows it as it plays or is dragged; the blue line only follows your pointer. [ and ] set the loop start and end at the bar the song is in. To move bar 1, type a time in the "bar 1 at" box, press Mark bar 1 while the song plays, or use Line up and tap the first word. Under Play, three sliders set the metronome, the sheet's notes, and the song apart from each other; the song slider drives Spotify's own volume. Tapping or typing a new tempo while the song plays never touches the song: the playhead just changes pace from where it is, and bar 1 shifts so the two stay matched. The first time you press Play on a song that has never been lined up, it plays the song and waits for you to press Space on the first word; after that Play runs the sheet with the song.

Listening

Attach a track under Play, press Line up, and press Space when you hear the first word: bar one lines itself up. Click anywhere on the staff to set where Play starts; the band parks there. Turn on "Click plays" under Play if you want the click to start playing at once. Ctrl+click two bars to loop them. Drag on the waveform to loop those bars, or Shift+[ and Shift+] at the band. \ turns the loop on or off. , and . step a beat (with Shift, a bar). Backspace rewinds two seconds. Shift+5 Shift+7 Shift+9 set 50, 75, 100 percent speed.

Loop lane

While a loop plays, D R I F S L T or the degrees 1 to 7 fill the syllables inside the loop in order and wrap around. + and - raise or lower the last one. The amber outline shows the next one. Tab skips, Backspace clears the last. Drum keys work the same in the Drums view.

Sing it

V or the Mic button listens. Sing a steady note and the pill names it in the key. Enter writes it into the lane syllable or the selected one. Pause the track first, or it hears the song too.
