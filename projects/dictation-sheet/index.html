Timing

Each bar is split into sixteenths. Drag a syllable on the strip, or drag its note left or right on the staff, and it snaps to the ticks on the timing lines; the blue band shows where it will land. Notes never overlap: whatever a dropped note runs into is pushed just far enough, and dropping inside an earlier note lands right after it. Whatever stays empty becomes a rest.

By hand

The plain way to write a song down: link or attach the song, set the tempo and the time signature in the clock, and set where the first note is played in the "bar 1 at" box under Play (or press Now while the song is there). The sheet then works out its bars on its own: the song's length from bar 1, cut into bars at that tempo, kept right whenever the tempo, the time signature, bar 1 or the song changes (Bars from the song redoes it by hand). A green line on the sheet shows where the song comes in. When the bars sit a beat or two off the song, drag the green line so the song's first beat lands on a bar line, or press the two arrows next to Now under Play to move it a beat at a time: the notes move with the song, the bar lines stay. The sheet may start before the song; Play then runs the empty bars and the song comes in at the green line. Press Start a line and pick Add (W). The red line is your cursor: ← and → move it by the step, ; makes the step smaller and ' bigger (sixteenth, eighth, beat, two beats, bar), and N puts a note where it stands; or click a bar where the word is sung. The note's box opens. Type the word; ↑ and ↓ move its pitch a step while you type (Shift for two); Enter keeps the word and opens the next note, an eighth, right after it, on the same pitch, so a line is typed word by word. Enter on an empty box ends the line. A - typed in the box cuts the word where the cursor is: the part before stays on this note, linked on, and the part after moves to a new note right after it, so "everything" becomes ev- ery- thing with two presses. ] and [ make it longer or shorter, 1 to 7 also set the pitch. The ↵ button at the end of a line starts a new line under it. Play it back against the song to check.

Tools

The tools run down the rail on the left, one symbol each; rest the pointer on one for its name. Edit, View and Play in the top row pick which set the rail shows, and a group's boxes and sliders open from the last icon of its group. Alt hides the rail and shows it again. W Add, M Move, E Edit, C Cut. Holding the right mouse button on a word drags it with Move whatever tool is active. Cut: click a word on the strip to split it where you clicked, or a note on the staff to split it in half. Right-click a word or note for a menu.

The note

Click a syllable or its notehead and the note turns blue and sounds (not while the band plays); a click on it again keeps it selected; a click anywhere on the sheet that is not on a note, or Escape, lets go; click it twice quickly to edit its word; the number keys set the degree and step on to the next note, so a line of existing notes is rewritten key by key; the status bar shows its pitch, degree, and length. Drag it up or down on the staff to set the pitch. In the Drums view the same drag moves it between the drum voices. 1 to 7 set the scale degree. In the Vocals view 1 is do and 7 is ti. In the other views 1 is the key's tonic, so in A minor 1 is A. Rests are the gaps between notes and never turn into notes. A click on a rest, or on any empty spot, puts the red line there, so Play starts from it (with Add, a note goes there). / holds the selected note into the rest after it. Double-click an empty spot on a bar to add a note there. Ctrl+← Ctrl+→ move it a sixteenth in time, Ctrl+Shift an eighth. Ctrl+↑ Ctrl+↓ move it one staff step, Ctrl+Shift two steps. Shift+↑ Shift+↓ add a sharp or flat. Alt+↑ Alt+↓ shift the octave. Or drag the notehead up and down the staff.

Length

Drag the right edge of a note on the strip, or press + for longer and - for shorter, through sixteenth, eighth, quarter, half, whole. / adds a sixteenth, . takes one away; with Ctrl they add or take an eighth. Dotted is in the right-click menu; 0 no note. A length with no single written value, like a quarter plus a sixteenth, draws as tied notes. A note can also be held past the bar line, up to four bars, and is written tied across it. Making a note longer pushes the notes after it later, never on top of them. Making it shorter leaves a gap, which is a rest. ] and [ make the selected note longer or shorter by the Snap step (Shift: a sixteenth); the note keeps its start and grows to the right.

Moving around

← → move to the word before or after, ↑ ↓ to the nearest word on the line above or below. Ctrl+← Ctrl+→ move the note in time by a sixteenth (into an empty bar at the end of the line before too; a note held on in front is shortened to make room); Ctrl+Shift+← Ctrl+Shift+→ slide it until it touches the nearest word that way (the first word of a line goes up to touch the last word of the line before); with a group highlighted they do the same for the group, Shift alone a sixteenth, Alt+[ and Alt+] an eighth. A moved note pushes only what it runs into. Alt+← Alt+→ move the whole rest of the line with it. Enter edits the text, Delete removes the word. Alt on its own opens and closes the tools drawer.

Groups of words

Drag across empty space on the strip to highlight every word the box touches (Shift adds to the group). Shift+click highlights every word from the selected one to the clicked one, Ctrl+click adds or takes away one. In Move, drag any highlighted word and the whole group slides with it; Ctrl+← Ctrl+→ nudge the group a Snap step; Ctrl+Shift slides it until it touches the nearest word. Sliding right pushes along what the group runs into; sliding left stops at the note before. Escape or a plain click lets the group go.

Shove

Alt+→ pushes the selected note and everything after it later by one Snap step. Alt+← pulls them earlier, stopping at the note before. Ctrl+Alt+← Ctrl+Alt+→ shove by a sixteenth, Ctrl+Alt+Shift by an eighth. Backspace removes up to a quarter of rest in front of the selected note; press it again for the next quarter.

Linked syllables

The syllables of one word stay linked: each has its own box on the strip, chained by their hyphens (Ev- ery- thing), and the word prints as con-se-quence under the notes on the staff. A box moves on its own; a note tied on to a syllable stays inside its box. Ctrl+Backspace on any note of the word, or Unlink in its right-click menu, takes the word apart into single notes. The Cut tool on a linked note frees just that one.

Solfege

D R I F S L T set do re mi fa sol la ti (I is mi, since M is the Move tool) and move on to the next syllable. The note spells itself from the key.

Drums

In the Drums view: K kick, N snare, A hat, O open hat, P pedal, H crash, I ride, T cycles the toms.

Place the words

This is the one path. Link the song under Play (Spotify, or attach its file), then press Place words. The app looks up the song's words from LRCLIB, a free open lyrics database, and hands them with the song's sound to a speech model on the desktop copy's local server, which fits the known words to the singing, sound by sound: every word is cut into its syllables from the CMU pronouncing dictionary, and every syllable gets the moment it is sung and the moment it stops. The beats of the song give the tempo, bar 1 goes on the downbeat before the first word, and the sheet follows the beats bar by bar; the gaps draw as rests. Which beat is beat 1 is read from where the lines start, the kick and bass, and the chord changes; when the song does not say clearly, bar 1 sits on the beat before the first word; dragging the green line on the sheet (or the two arrows next to Now under Play, a beat at a time) then moves the song under the bars, the notes staying with the song. With an attached file this runs at once (a minute or two for a whole song, the bottom bar counts). With a Spotify song the song plays through first while the server records the sound card, then the fit runs; the sound is kept only in memory for that run. Ctrl+Z takes the whole fill back. Then Move or Edit fixes any syllable that landed wrong, and the pitches are yours. The tools behind it are installed once with pip install torch torchaudio librosa soundfile cmudict pyphen (the first run also fetches the speech model, about 360 MB); without them the app falls back to reading the song's loudness, which is far rougher.

Tempo

Place words sets the tempo from the song. Without it, type a tempo or press Tap in the clock button; more taps, tighter number. The sheet keeps decimals, since a whole-number tempo can be a full beat off by the end of a song. A small percent badge on the clock shows when the band runs slower than the song.

Tuning

The key button holds the tuning: A4 is 440 Hz unless you change it, and every played note keeps its ratio to that A, so a sheet at 432 or 442 plays true. Hear A sounds the reference.

Drone

Drone in the top row holds a steady low tone on the key's home note (do in the Vocals view, the tonic in the others) with its octave and a soft fifth, to sing against. While it is on, a slider beside the button sets its volume. It follows the key, the view and the tuning, and stops when pressed again.

Note pad

Pad in the top row, or P, pulls up ten keys in a row along the scale, each with its degree, syllable and note name, so a view can hold two of most degrees. While it is open the top letter row plays them: Q the far-left key through P the far right (these letters then play the pad instead of picking tools or syllables), and Shift+P closes it. The arrows on either side slide the row one note down or up (Shift: an octave). Press a key to hear it and check a note you are singing; with a note selected on the sheet, a pad key (click or letter) also writes that pitch to the note, degree and octave, and steps on to the next note; while the pad is open the number keys 1 to 7 sound the lowest key in view with that degree and 8 the next 1 up (with a note selected, 1 to 7 still set its degree as well). In the Vocals view 1 is do; in the other views 1 is the key's tonic. Shift with a press sounds a key an octave down, Alt an octave up. It follows the key and the tuning.

Views

Pick the instrument in the top row, or press Tab to cycle through them (Shift+Tab backwards). Vocals shows solfege over the notes. Guitar shows the scale degree, 1 to 7, over each note and the note name in the key under the staff. Bass shows note names with tab. Piano shows both hands. Drums shows the drum staff with rhythm syllables over each hit: ta ka di mi for the four sixteenths of a beat, ta di for eighths.

Play

Space plays or pauses. Select a word and the red line parks half a bar before it, so Play starts with a lead-in to that word. Click a bar on the sheet to set where Play starts instead; while playing, a click jumps there. The red line is where the song is; the page keeps it in view. Metronome clicks every beat when on. - starts a loop at the red line, a bar long, drawn as an orange band with a bracket at each end; drag the end bracket (or the start one) to where the loop should run, and - again removes it. Ctrl+click two bars also loops them; [ and ] with no note selected set the loop at the bar the song is in, \ turns it on or off, and a click outside it turns it off. , and . step a beat (with Shift, a bar). Backspace rewinds two seconds. Shift+5 Shift+7 Shift+9 set 50, 75, 100 percent speed (Spotify always plays at full speed). Under Play, three sliders set the metronome, the sheet's notes, and the song apart from each other, and Notes only (♩) makes the sheet play its notes while the song stays silent; press it again for the song. Notes and Audio in the top row are the same two switches, one at a time.

Spotify

On the desktop copy: make a free app at developer.spotify.com with the redirect URI http://127.0.0.1:8765/callback, paste its Client ID under Play, press Connect, log in, then type the song's name in the search box and press Find. Pick it from the list (or paste a Spotify link instead) and it becomes the sheet's song with Audio switched on, so Play drives your Spotify at the right spot while the band follows it. Premium is required. Once a song is linked a strip appears under the tabs: a click on it puts the song at that spot, the red line is where the song is and can be dragged, and the blue flag is where bar 1 of the sheet starts. The app listens to the song as it plays through the app, so Place words can hear where the words are sung; this needs two Python packages, installed once with pip install pyaudiowpatch numpy.
