# Monarc Edge backtest, 2026-10-04

Plain words first. This is the proof behind the model: we pretended to be on each matchweek from 2019/20 to 2025/26, refitted the model on the matches played before that week, and wrote down our three chances for every match before looking at the result. Then we scored those chances against what happened, and against the sharpest public price (Pinnacle's closing line, with the bookmaker's margin taken out), and ran the live betting rules against that line as if it were Kalshi.

**What to expect, stated before the numbers.** A stats-only model with no market input usually lands within 1 to 2 points of log loss of the closing line and does not beat it. Against Pinnacle's closing line the simulated record is likely flat or negative, because that line already knows everything the model knows and more. The edge, if there is one, is where Kalshi's retail book differs from the sharp line, and only live cards can show that. What follows is what we actually got.

## The short version

- Accuracy: the model's log loss is 0.9833 against the closing line's 0.9583 over 2490 matches. The market is sharper by 0.020, inside the 1 to 2 point range a public-data model usually lands in.
- Calibration: 9 of 10 bands pass with slope 0.96 (the market: 9 of 10, slope 0.99). When the model says 40%, it happens about 40% of the time.
- The record against the closing line: 625 bets, 33% hit, -$6,496.27 after fees, -14.8% on cash. Random bets with the same stakes return -5.8%. So where the model disagrees with Pinnacle's close by 7 points or more, Pinnacle is right more often than chance: the gap picks out the model's worst errors, not its insights.
- Max drawdown $6,817.15: a real $1,000 bankroll would have gone bust following these rules against the closing line. The flat-bankroll simulation keeps betting so the whole record can be read; a live bankroll would not.
- CLV at the pre-close price: -2.52 points, 20% positive. The line did not move toward the sides the model liked.
- No-look-ahead canary: PASS. Runtime 16 s with the per-week cache warm; a cold run is about 100 s on this laptop.
- What it means for the plan: this model is a fair baseline and a working Stage 3 and 4, not an edge over a sharp book. Any edge has to come from Kalshi's price sitting away from the sharp line; the live cards measure that, the backtest cannot.

## The setup

- Data: football-data.co.uk E0, seasons on disk 2015/16, 2016/17, 2017/18, 2018/19, 2019/20, 2020/21, 2021/22, 2022/23, 2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26, 2026/27. Pinnacle closing odds present in 2015/16, 2016/17, 2017/18, 2018/19, 2019/20, 2020/21, 2021/22, 2022/23, 2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26 (partial: 2025/26 55% of rows, 2026/27 0% of rows; Pinnacle left the feed on 2026-01-08, so 2025/26 is scored for accuracy on every match and against the market on the rows that have the line).
- Scored: 2660 matches over 7 seasons, 2490 with a closing line.
- Walk-forward seasons (also used to pick the tuning numbers): 2016/17, 2017/18, 2018/19, 2019/20, 2020/21, 2021/22, 2022/23, 2023/24, 2024/25, 2025/26.
- Model: Dixon-Coles Poisson goals model (attack and defence per team, home advantage, the rho fix for low-score draws), time decay exp(-xi x days), shots on target blended in through a second likelihood. Weak ridge toward the promoted-team average for new teams. Score grid to 10 goals a side.
- Tuning, nested: xi from {0.001, 0.002, 0.004} and sot_blend from {0, 0.5}, picked for each test season on the seasons before it by predictive log loss. Temperature fitted on the previous season's out-of-sample chances.
- Rules: bet when our chance beats the price by 7 points after the 7% taker fee; stake 6% of the bankroll at the floor rising to 8% at 17 points; one bet per match (the largest gap of the six sides); flat $1,000 bankroll (stakes never compound).
- Runtime: 16 s.

## (b) Calibration by band

Each match gives three rows (its home, draw and away chance). A band holds every stated chance in that ten-point range. 'Stated' is the average chance we said; 'Hit' is how often it happened; the interval is the Wilson 95% range the hit rate could really be. A band passes when the stated chance sits inside that interval and within 5 points of the hit rate. A slope of 1.0 means a point of stated chance was worth a point of real chance; under 1.0 means over-confident, over 1.0 timid.

## (c) The simulated bet record

Pinnacle's closing fair chance stands in for Kalshi: we pay fair plus one cent (half a two-cent spread) for a Yes, and one minus (fair minus one cent) for a No, plus the taker fee. One bet per match, the largest gap of the six sides, only at 7 points or more. CLV (closing line value) per bet = the closing fair chance of the side we bought minus our break-even price, in points. In this simulation the closing line is also the price we paid, so CLV is negative by the spread and fee by construction (about -2.7 points); the pre-close variant below is the real CLV test.

| Bets | Hit rate | Cash down | P&L after fees | Return on cash | Fees | Max drawdown | Mean gap | Mean CLV | CLV > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 625 | 33.3% | $43,764.27 | -$6,496.27 | -14.8% | $1,879.54 | $6,817.15 (681.7% of bankroll) | 10.8 | -2.50 | 0% |

Bootstrap (the bet list resampled 10,000 times): return on cash between -23.6% and -6.0% nine times in ten (median -15.0%); 0% of resamples end positive.

Random control (same number of bets, same stakes, a random side of a random match, 1,000 times): mean return -5.8%, 90% range -14.2% to +2.7%. That is what paying the spread and fee with no skill costs.

## What this does and does not say

- The model is a fair public-data baseline. It is not sharper than Pinnacle's close and was never expected to be.
- Stage 4 (does the win rate scale with the stated chance) is the calibration table above; it is the thing the live gate re-checks on real cards.
- Stage 5's green or red percent is the gap column of the live card. In this backtest the gap against the closing line was mostly model error; the live question is whether Kalshi's price sits far enough from the sharp line often enough.
- Live data from API-Football (possession, tackles and interceptions, take-ons, lineups) is not in this model yet; edge_features.py has the slots for it.
- The temperature and the two tuning numbers are the only things fitted across seasons; each season only ever saw its own past.
